Google Ads Video Creative: A Campaign-Ready Checklist for Small Businesses

Choose the campaign goal first, then produce the formats, messages and handoff assets the media plan actually needs.

Published November 6, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial8 min read

A polished video is not automatically a campaign-ready video ad. The business objective, Google Ads campaign subtype, placement mix, aspect ratios, copy, landing page and conversion measurement all affect what the production team should deliver. Use this checklist before filming so one shoot produces useful assets instead of a single edit that fits only one placement.

Start with one measurable campaign objective

Google Ads currently guides Video campaigns around objectives that include Sales, Leads, Website traffic, and YouTube reach, views and engagements. Google also notes that a Video campaign’s marketing objective cannot be edited after the campaign is created.[1]

Translate that objective into one viewer action before writing the script. A lead campaign might ask a qualified viewer to book a consultation; a website-traffic campaign might send people to one service page; a reach campaign may prioritize a memorable message rather than an immediate form submission. The business video brief helps document the audience, offer, proof and call to action.

Choose the campaign subtype before the edit list

The objective influences the campaign subtype, available ad formats, networks and bidding options. Google’s current Video campaign guidance includes video views, efficient reach, non-skippable reach, target frequency, ad sequence and other subtypes under its reach, views and engagement objective.[2]

Do not ask the editor for “a YouTube ad” without confirming the intended campaign setup. A six-second bumper, a skippable in-stream ad, an in-feed video and a vertical Shorts placement solve different creative problems. The media buyer and producer should agree on the required formats, lengths and destination before production begins.

Plan a family of cuts, not one universal master

Google’s published specifications support horizontal 16:9, vertical 9:16 and square 1:1 video assets across several campaign types. Its current specification page recommends 1920 × 1080 for horizontal, 1080 × 1920 for vertical and 1080 × 1080 for square assets in the formats it lists.[3]

Frame the shoot so the subject, product, logo and call to action survive all required crops. Capture wider safety on key shots and avoid placing essential text at the extreme edges. Build each cut for its placement rather than centring a horizontal edit inside a vertical canvas with unused space.

Match length to the format and message

Google describes bumper ads as non-skippable ads lasting between five and six seconds. It also says non-skippable in-stream ads longer than 30 seconds in auction campaigns are not allowed, while videos under three minutes typically perform better for skippable in-stream ads.[4][5]

Treat these as format constraints, not storytelling targets. A six-second bumper needs one idea and immediate branding. A longer skippable ad can establish a problem, demonstrate proof and present an offer, but it still needs a clear opening. Produce alternate openings and calls to action when the campaign will test different audiences or offers.

Make the advertiser and offer clear

Google’s video ad requirements say the video must include a name or logo that clearly represents the advertiser, product or service. Video ads also need to meet Google Ads policies, quality requirements and copyright rules.[5]

Show the brand early enough that the viewer can identify the source, but do not let a logo animation consume the entire opening. State the offer in plain language, support claims with evidence and secure permission for music, footage, locations and people. Avoid promises the landing page or sales process cannot substantiate.

Prepare headlines, descriptions and calls to action

The upload is more than a video file. For the Video action specifications it lists, Google currently allows a headline up to 30 characters, a long headline up to 90 characters, a description up to 90 characters and a call to action up to 10 characters.[3] Other formats have different limits.

Write the copy while the video is being approved, not at the launch screen. Keep a reviewed copy sheet with character counts, final URLs, tracking parameters and a clear owner. Ensure the text and video make the same offer and use the same terminology.

Deliver thumbnails and supporting images where required

In-feed video ads use a thumbnail and text to invite people to watch. Google’s specification page lists a 16:9 thumbnail at 1280 × 720 pixels, with a minimum of 1280 × 640, in JPG, GIF or PNG format and under 2 MB for videos.[3]

Select the thumbnail intentionally. It should communicate the subject at small size, remain accurate and avoid duplicating interface-like buttons that could mislead viewers. Include the thumbnail and any companion assets in the final delivery folder with clear names.

Align the landing page with the ad

The landing page should continue the promise made in the video. Repeat the offer, show the relevant proof and make the intended action obvious. Send viewers to the most specific useful page rather than the homepage when a dedicated service or campaign page exists.

Check the final URL on mobile, confirm that the form or booking action works and use approved campaign parameters when analytics requires them. A strong view rate cannot compensate for a slow, confusing or mismatched destination.

Set measurement before launch

Google’s campaign workflow asks advertisers to choose targeting, bidding, budget and ads around the selected goal, and it allows multiple ads in a campaign.[6] Decide before launch which business action matters and which creative variables will be compared.

Keep tests interpretable: change one major variable at a time, such as the opening, offer, proof point or call to action. Document the version names in both the delivery folder and the ad account. Use the business video KPI framework to separate attention metrics from website actions and qualified business outcomes.

Campaign-ready delivery checklist

  • The campaign objective and primary viewer action are written down.
  • The campaign subtype and intended placements are confirmed.
  • Required horizontal, vertical and square versions are listed.
  • Each format has an appropriate length and opening.
  • The advertiser, product or service is clearly identifiable.
  • Claims, music, footage and participant permissions are reviewed.
  • Headlines, descriptions, calls to action and final URLs are approved.
  • Required thumbnails and supporting images are exported.
  • The landing page matches the ad and works on mobile.
  • Conversion tracking and campaign parameters are checked.
  • Creative variants have clear names and one documented difference.
  • Final assets are reviewed against current Google specifications and policies.
