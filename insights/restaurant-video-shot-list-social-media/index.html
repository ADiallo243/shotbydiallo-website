Restaurant Video Shot List: 18 Essential Shots for Social Media

Capture the room, food, people and service in one organized production.

Published October 30, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial8 min read

A useful restaurant shoot does more than collect beautiful plates. It builds a library that can explain where the restaurant is, what the room feels like, how a signature dish comes together and what a guest can expect. The shot list below is designed for a focused production that can support several short edits rather than one isolated post.

Plan the outputs before the camera arrives

Decide where the footage will be used and which messages matter most. TikTok’s current creative guidance recommends vertical 9:16 framing, high-resolution footage, space for interface elements and a hook-body-close structure for ads.[1] Google Maps separately recommends media captured at the actual location, with the place as the main subject, and says stable, in-focus video is most helpful.[2]

Those requirements are not interchangeable. Capture clean vertical masters for social media, plus wider or simpler location footage when the restaurant also needs website, press or Google Business Profile assets.

1–4. Establish the place

  1. Exterior and sign. Record a stable wide shot that makes the entrance recognizable.
  2. Approach to the door. Show the route a first-time guest will take from sidewalk to entrance.
  3. Empty-room wide. Film before service so the layout, lighting and design are visible.
  4. Atmosphere with guests. Capture a controlled service moment that shows the room in use without relying on identifiable customers who have not agreed to appear.

Google’s own Maps video tips recommend capturing a location’s atmosphere, exterior and useful details.[3] For a restaurant, these first shots answer the practical question: “What will it feel like to arrive?”

5–8. Show the people and process

  1. Chef or cook at work. Use a real preparation step rather than a staged pose.
  2. One precise hand movement. Knife work, plating, pouring or garnishing makes a strong short-form detail.
  3. Front-of-house preparation. Capture table setting, glass polishing, menu placement or the opening routine.
  4. Service handoff. Film the moment a finished plate or drink moves from the pass to the dining room.

Keep the action repeatable and safe. Agree on the movement before recording, avoid blocking the team during a rush and collect several clean variations of the same action for different edits.

9–13. Build a food and drink library

  1. Signature dish hero. Give one plate its own uncluttered close-up.
  2. Ingredient detail. Show a texture, seasonal ingredient or locally sourced component that supports a real menu story.
  3. Transformation. Record a before-and-after sequence from raw element to finished dish.
  4. Drink build. Capture ice, pour, garnish and the final glass as separate usable moments.
  5. Table spread. Film an overhead or angled view showing how several items work together.

Google’s Maps guidance specifically suggests highlighting signature dishes, action around food and either one dish per video or a clear view of a full table.[3] Treat each dish as a distinct asset so it can be used alone without confusing the viewer.

14–16. Capture the guest experience

  1. Menu decision. Show hands opening the menu or pointing to a selection.
  2. First interaction. Record a greeting, water pour or plate arrival with participants who have agreed to be filmed.
  3. Reaction without performance. A natural hand-to-dish moment, toast or shared-table action can communicate enjoyment without asking for an exaggerated testimonial.

Google’s Maps content policy identifies a person’s face in a photo or video as personal information when posted without consent.[4] Plan releases or other appropriate permission for identifiable guests and staff. This is a production safeguard, not legal advice.

17–18. Finish with flexible brand assets

  1. Clean logo or sign detail. Record enough hold time for an editor to use it as an ending.
  2. One clear next step. Film a reservation book, pickup counter, terrace or event setup that supports the intended call to action.

Do not force every phone number, offer and menu claim into the frame. A clean visual ending leaves room for platform-native captions and buttons, and it is easier to update when hours or promotions change.

How to turn 18 shots into several posts

  • Atmosphere edit: exterior, room, guests, service handoff and sign.
  • Signature dish edit: ingredient, preparation, transformation and final plate.
  • People edit: chef, front of house, service and one short spoken line.
  • Drink edit: build, garnish, table placement and reaction.
  • Google Business Profile cut: a simple location-focused edit that stays within Google’s current limit of 30 seconds, 75 MB and 720p or higher.[5]

The shot list is a starting point, not a guarantee of performance. Publish different hooks and edits, compare the platform’s own results and keep the combinations that help the restaurant communicate clearly.

Pre-shoot checklist

  • Confirm the priority dishes, drinks and service moments.
  • Choose a quiet setup window and a realistic service window.
  • Prepare fresh hero plates specifically for filming.
  • Clean the entrance, tables, glassware and visible work areas.
  • Confirm who is authorized and comfortable appearing on camera.
  • List the required vertical, horizontal and Google-ready deliverables.
  • Check current prices, menu names, hours and reservation details before adding text.
  • Assign one restaurant contact to approve factual details during the shoot.
