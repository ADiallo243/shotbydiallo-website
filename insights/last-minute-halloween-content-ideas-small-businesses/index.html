Last-Minute Halloween Content Ideas for Small Businesses

Seven useful, low-budget Halloween content ideas a small business can still create and publish before October 31.

Published October 28, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial7 min read

With Halloween only days away, the best content is simple, specific and already available inside the business. Skip an elaborate campaign. Make one useful or entertaining idea, film it cleanly, and connect it to something customers can still do before October 31.

1. Turn a normal process into a Halloween transformation

Show the ordinary version first, then a seasonal variation: a restaurant plates a Halloween special, a florist changes a neutral arrangement, or a salon moves from everyday styling to a costume-ready look. Keep the transformation genuine and achievable. The reveal works because the viewer understands the change immediately.

Simple shot plan: one locked-off “before,” three close-ups of the process and the matching “after.” Add the offer or availability in the caption instead of covering the video with text.

2. Answer the urgent question customers are asking this week

A bakery can explain the last pickup time. A family venue can clarify whether costumes are welcome. A restaurant can show what remains available without a reservation. Put the answer in the first sentence and repeat the practical detail in the caption.

This is more useful than a generic “Happy Halloween” post because it removes friction while there is still time to act.

3. Make a staff-choice carousel or quick-cut video

Ask each team member one easy question: favourite candy, film, costume or local Halloween tradition. Film each answer in the same place and keep it to one sentence. The repetition gives the piece structure; the different answers keep it human.

If nobody wants to appear on camera, photograph or film the choices with handwritten name cards. Participation should be voluntary, and the post should still make sense without pressuring staff into costumes.

4. Create a “choose one” product or service matchup

Pair two relevant options and invite a clear choice: spicy versus sweet, classic versus unusual, subtle versus full costume. Show the actual products or outcomes. Avoid engagement bait with no relationship to the business—the prompt should help people discover what you sell.

5. Publish a neighbourhood mini-guide

Share three nearby stops that complement the customer’s visit: where to grab coffee before an event, a family-friendly activity, or a practical parking or transit note. Confirm every detail before publishing and tag only businesses genuinely included. For Montréal audiences, write the guide in the language your customers use; add both languages when your team can review them naturally.

6. Show a low-cost behind-the-scenes preparation

Film window decorating, packaging, menu prep or the final checklist before an event. The story is the work, not the decoration budget. Record natural sound, use a short voice-over to explain one decision, and end with the finished space.

When using TikTok for a brand or product, the platform recommends music from its Commercial Music Library because music outside that library may not be licensed for commercial content.[1] Original sound—staff narration, kitchen sound, packaging or room tone—is often the simplest last-minute option.

7. Offer one genuinely useful reminder

Useful reminders travel well: ordering cutoffs, weather contingencies, accessibility, age suitability, allergy information, closing time or what to bring. Verify the detail, put it on screen in large type and update the post if circumstances change.

A 45-minute production plan

  1. 10 minutes: choose one idea, write the opening line and confirm the facts.
  2. 15 minutes: capture one wide view, three details and a clean ending.
  3. 10 minutes: record a short explanation or natural sound.
  4. 10 minutes: edit, caption, proof the date and publish.

Shoot vertically and leave clear space around important text and faces. TikTok’s current creative guidance calls for 9:16 framing, high-resolution footage and room for the interface.[2]

What to avoid this late

  • A contest whose rules, prize or selection process are not ready
  • A discount the team cannot honour consistently
  • A trend that requires explaining why it relates to the business
  • Costumes, music or footage the business does not have permission to use
  • Publishing an event detail without checking the organizer’s current information

Last-minute seasonal content should lower the production burden, not transfer confusion to customers. One clear piece with a real deadline or useful answer is enough.
