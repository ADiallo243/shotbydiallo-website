Instagram Reels Ads for Small Businesses: A Video Production Checklist

Plan the campaign idea, full-screen frame, sound, safe zones and export before a Reels ad goes into review.

Published November 27, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial8 min read

A paid Reels placement has specific creative and file requirements. Meta’s current Ads Guide recommends a 9:16 vertical video and sets technical guidance for resolution, file type, sound and safe areas. This guide translates that ad-specific documentation into a production handoff for small teams. It is about preparing a reviewable asset; it does not promise reach, conversions or ad approval.

Separate an ad export from an organic Reel

Start by writing down the actual destination: Instagram Reels ad placement, the campaign objective, the conversion location and the action the viewer should take. Meta’s Ads Guide treats Reels ads as full-screen vertical placements inserted between organic Reels, and shows that available objectives and conversion locations vary by setup.[1]

Do not assume an organic post that looks right on a phone is automatically ready for paid delivery. Paid creative needs an ad-specific export review in Ads Manager, along with a check of the current campaign and placement settings. If the same footage will become an organic post too, label and review those as separate versions.

Write one clear job for the video

Choose one audience and one action before scripting. A neighbourhood restaurant might invite people to view a seasonal menu; a service company might explain one decision and lead to a consultation page; a product brand might demonstrate one real use. The opening should make the subject and value understandable without requiring a long introduction.

Build a compact sequence: show the product, place or person; demonstrate the point; give the context needed to trust it; then make the next step clear. Keep claims tied to evidence the business can substantiate. If the ad promotes a price, deadline, result or offer, confirm the details and destination page before recording the call to action.

Compose for the full-screen vertical frame

Meta lists MP4 and MOV as supported file types for Instagram Reels ads, recommends a 9:16 ratio and 1440 × 2560 resolution, and describes video settings including H.264 compression, square pixels, fixed frame rate, progressive scan and stereo AAC audio at 128 kbps or higher.[1] Treat these as the current platform recommendations for this placement, then confirm the requirements shown in the account where the ad will run.

Compose while filming rather than relying on a centre crop after the shoot. Keep the main action, face, product label and any essential visual evidence inside the vertical frame. Leave room for interface controls and captions. Capture a clean master so the editor can make a separate crop if the campaign also needs Feed, Stories or another placement; do not stretch or crop the only approved export into every format.

Keep key information inside Meta’s safe area

Meta’s Reels ad guidance suggests leaving at least 14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% on each side free of text, logos and other important creative elements. These margins help keep key material away from controls, profile icons, calls to action and screen edges.[1]

Use the figures as a layout guide, not as a substitute for previewing the actual asset. Keep subtitles, product names, prices and the visual proof away from the lower interface area. Meta’s Reels lesson says Ads Manager can be used to check that creative fits within the safe zone; use that preview on the campaign version before final approval.[2]

Plan sound, captions and music together

Meta marks video sound as optional but strongly recommended, and captions as optional but recommended for this placement.[1] Make dialogue intelligible, balance music beneath speech, and prepare reviewed captions for spoken content. The W3C explains that captions include the speech and non-speech audio information needed to understand video, synchronized with the audio.[3]

Check music rights specifically for advertising. Meta’s Reels ad guide says ads should not contain licensed music and recommends original audio or royalty-free music from Meta Sound Collection.[1] Confirm that a chosen track is available and permitted for the account, country and intended paid use; a song that was available for an organic post is not automatically cleared for an advertisement.

Build the edit and export checklist

The Meta Ads Guide lists an ad duration up to 15 minutes, a maximum file size of 4 GB, and minimum width of 250 pixels for ads under 30 seconds or 500 pixels for ads 30 seconds or longer.[1] A maximum is an eligibility boundary, not a creative target. Choose a runtime that is long enough to explain the offer and short enough to avoid repeating the same point.

  • Keep the approved master and paid placement export as separate files.
  • Check the ratio, dimensions, codec, audio settings, duration and file size against the current Ads Guide and Ads Manager.
  • Review captions, names, prices, dates, claims and calls to action against the approved brief.
  • Confirm that the final destination page matches the spoken and written offer.
  • Preview the ad in Ads Manager and watch the exported file on a phone with sound on and off.
  • Check that no required text, face, product detail or logo is covered by the interface.
  • Confirm music and appearance permissions cover the intended paid placement.

Make creative variations deliberate

If the campaign needs more than one version, vary a meaningful creative choice: the opening question, demonstration, speaker, proof point or call to action. Keep the offer and destination consistent when the goal is to compare those choices. Name versions clearly and record which one received approval. Do not make unreviewed changes to price, deadline, testimonial language or product claims between exports.

This gives the media buyer assets that are easy to distinguish and gives the business a clear record of what was approved. It also keeps the video-production handoff separate from campaign decisions such as audience, budget and placement.

Final Reels ad production checklist

  • The campaign objective, conversion location and next action are documented.
  • The video has one audience, one message and a supportable offer.
  • The composition is designed for 9:16 rather than cropped as an afterthought.
  • Text, logos and important visual details stay within Meta’s suggested safe area.
  • Dialogue is clear, music is balanced, and captions have been reviewed.
  • The music and appearances are cleared for the planned paid use.
  • The export is checked against current Meta Ads Guide specifications.
  • The actual ad preview has been reviewed in Ads Manager.
  • The destination page, offer and final call to action agree.
  • The exact final version and its approval are recorded before launch.
