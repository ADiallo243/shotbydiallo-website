Influencer and Creator Videos: A Canadian Disclosure Checklist

Plan creator partnerships with clear disclosures, supportable claims and a documented review process before publishing.

Published November 17, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial8 min read

A creator video can feel informal while still being advertising. Canadian businesses should identify material connections, plan a disclosure viewers will actually notice and review both the creator’s claims and the complete presentation before the post goes live.

Identify every material connection at the brief stage

Start by documenting what the creator, employee or affiliate receives and what relationship exists. Payment is not the only connection that can matter.

The Competition Bureau says material connections can include money or commissions, free products or services, discounts, trips, event tickets and personal or family relationships.[1] Ad Standards’ interpretation guidance also identifies employee, family, affiliate and refer-a-friend relationships as connections that may require disclosure.[2]

Choose disclosure words people will understand immediately

Write the disclosure into the creative brief rather than leaving it to the upload screen. Use plain language that explains the nature of the relationship. Avoid shorthand that viewers may not understand.

The Competition Bureau recommends visible, prominent disclosures using clear and contextually appropriate language. It warns that a long caption, group of hashtags, profile bio, brand tag, discount code or affiliate link alone may not be enough.[1] Test the wording with someone who was not involved in the campaign: can they tell immediately that the content has a commercial connection?

Put the disclosure where the audience experiences the claim

Place disclosure close to the recommendation, not after a long story or behind an expansion control. For short vertical video, plan it inside the opening moments and keep it readable long enough to register.

Ad Standards’ Interpretation Guideline #5 says a material connection should be disclosed clearly, prominently and in close proximity to the representation.[2] The Competition Bureau also says every platform needs its own disclosure and that some content may need both audio and visual disclosure.[1]

Use platform tools, but do not rely on them alone

Turn on the platform’s paid-partnership or paid-promotion setting when it applies. Then keep the human-readable disclosure inside the content or caption as required by the campaign context.

YouTube requires creators to select the paid-promotion box when a video contains paid product placement, sponsorship, endorsement or other content requiring disclosure. YouTube also states that creators and brands remain responsible for local legal disclosure obligations.[3] Ad Standards’ current guidance includes using platform partnership tools effectively while maintaining clear disclosure practices.[4]

Match the disclosure to each format and edit

A disclosure prepared for a long YouTube video may not survive a 15-second cut, Story, Reel, Short or paid ad. Review every version independently.

Keep the disclosure attached when a video is downloaded, embedded, shared or reposted. The Competition Bureau’s influencer checklist recommends disclosures that are inseparable from the content so they travel with it when shared.[5] Include the disclosure in clean exports intended for partners, not only in the original platform caption.

Protect genuine experience and creator voice

Do not script a personal experience the creator did not have. Give accurate product information and required limitations, then allow the creator to express a genuine opinion in their own language.

The Competition Bureau says reviews and testimonials should be based on actual experience and creators should avoid broad performance claims that may lack testing or proof.[1] Separate a subjective reaction from an objective claim about results, performance or comparison.

Give every objective claim an evidence owner

Create a claims sheet before filming. List spoken, written and visual claims, their evidence, conditions and approver. Include demonstrations, before-and-after edits, pricing, savings, environmental statements and superlatives.

A disclosure explains the relationship; it does not make an unsupported claim acceptable. The Competition Bureau notes that businesses share responsibility for promotional messages and should review influencer content carefully.[6] Use the business video script review checklist for a structured claims and visual-impression pass.

Plan French disclosure for Québec audiences

When content is intended for the Québec market, include disclosure wording in the language version the audience receives. Do not assume an English hashtag or platform label is the only language work required.

Review the complete package: spoken disclosure, on-screen label, captions, post copy and landing page. Apply the French-first social video workflow to scripting, on-screen text and approvals, and obtain qualified advice where language requirements are uncertain.

Review accessibility and safe placement

Make the disclosure available to people who watch without sound and to people who cannot see the screen. If the connection is communicated in speech, caption it accurately. If it appears visually, consider whether an audio disclosure is also needed for the format and audience.

Keep disclosure text away from interface controls, ensure readable contrast and allow enough screen time. Review the actual uploaded preview, not only the editing timeline. The accessible social video checklist covers captions, contrast, meaningful audio and motion in more detail.

Define approval roles without erasing authenticity

The agreement should identify who checks disclosure, factual claims, brand safety, rights and final publication. The creator should know which changes are required for accuracy or compliance and which are suggestions.

Use one consolidated review rather than conflicting comments from several stakeholders. Record the approved disclosure wording and the exact final version. If the caption, crop, duration, destination or commercial relationship changes, run the disclosure review again.

Archive the campaign record

Keep the brief, agreement, material connection, product information, claims evidence, approval notes, final files, captions, post copy and live URLs together. Record where and when each version was published.

If the business later boosts an organic creator post, edits the video into an ad or licenses it to another channel, confirm that the disclosure still appears clearly in the new context. Do not treat approval for one post as automatic approval for every future use.

Influencer and creator video disclosure checklist

  • Every payment, gift, discount, commission, trip, ticket, employee, family or affiliate connection is documented.
  • The disclosure uses plain language the intended audience will understand.
  • The nature of the connection is clear, not merely the brand name.
  • The disclosure appears prominently and close to the recommendation.
  • Audio and visual disclosure are used when the format requires both.
  • Each platform and each edited version has its own disclosure check.
  • Applicable paid-partnership or paid-promotion tools are enabled.
  • The disclosure stays with downloaded, shared and reposted video.
  • The creator’s opinion reflects actual experience.
  • Objective claims have evidence, conditions and an approver.
  • The complete words-and-pictures impression has been reviewed.
  • French and English versions are approved for their intended audiences.
  • Captions, contrast, timing and interface safe zones are checked.
  • Brand and creator approval roles are defined.
  • The final files, copy, approvals and live URLs are archived.
