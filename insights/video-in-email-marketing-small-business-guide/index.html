Video in Email Marketing: A Practical Guide for Small Businesses

Make the message, thumbnail and click-through destination work together—without depending on a video player inside the inbox.

Published November 25, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial7 min read

A business video can support a launch, explain a service or bring a customer story into an email campaign. The reliable production plan is usually not “put a player in the email.” Mailchimp notes that most email clients do not support embedded video, and some strip the code. Its own video blocks instead use a linked thumbnail that opens the video elsewhere.[1] Build the email around a clear preview and a destination page that works when images or playback features do not.

Use the email as the invitation, not the video player

Design the message as a simple path: a short explanation, a representative preview image, and a clear link to watch. A linked still or thumbnail is familiar, light to load, and easier to test across email clients than embedded playback. The image should lead to a public page where the video can play in a standard browser or app.

This is a practical compatibility choice, not a promise that every image or link will render identically. Email clients handle HTML differently, so preview and test the campaign in the inboxes your audience uses. Mailchimp’s video block can automatically create a thumbnail for supported Vimeo and long-form YouTube URLs; other formats may need a manually added image.[2]

Choose a thumbnail that tells the truth

Use a frame that accurately represents the video: the product in use, the person speaking, the space being introduced, or a moment from the story. Keep the image readable at a narrow mobile width. A simple play symbol can suggest that the image is clickable, but do not let the symbol cover a face, product detail or important on-screen text.

Write the subject line, preview text and sentence above the thumbnail so they explain what the recipient will see and why it matters. “Watch our 45-second walkthrough of the new ordering process” sets a clearer expectation than “Watch now.” Keep the call to action specific, and avoid putting essential information only inside the image.

Make the click useful even when images are off

Email images may be blocked or unavailable, and people using screen readers need a text alternative. Add concise alt text to a linked thumbnail that describes the image’s purpose in context. Mailchimp recommends meaningful alt text because it can help recipients understand an image when it cannot be seen, including when a screen reader is used.[3]

Also place an ordinary text link or button near the thumbnail, such as “Watch the product walkthrough.” That gives the reader another way to continue if the image does not display or its linked-image behavior is unclear. Keep the email’s core message in live text, not baked into a graphic.

Send people to a page prepared for the campaign

The destination should deliver what the email promised. Before the campaign goes out, check that the page is public to the intended audience, the video starts in the expected place, the title and surrounding copy identify the subject, and the next action is easy to find. If the email promotes a service, include a relevant contact or booking action. If it shares a longer story, give the viewer a brief introduction and a clear next step.

Decide where the video will live before editing. A dedicated page on your website can keep the viewer close to your service information; a video platform may be simpler when the goal is to share a hosted video. Check access settings, playback on a phone, captions and the page’s privacy before using the link. If you are planning a batch of campaign outputs from one shoot, the one-shoot content planning guide can help you map the versions and destinations together.

Measure the email click and the viewing experience separately

Track the email link with your normal campaign analytics, then review what happens on the watch page. A click tells you that someone followed the link; it does not by itself show whether they watched the video or took the next action. Where your tools support it, compare email clicks with watch-page activity and the campaign’s intended outcome, using consistent link tags and a clearly defined measurement window. The business video KPI framework explains how to connect attention, viewing quality and meaningful next steps without treating views as the whole result.

Avoid promising that adding video will increase opens or sales. The format is one part of the message. The audience, subject line, timing, offer, thumbnail, destination and follow-through all matter, and a campaign should be evaluated against its own goal.

Test the complete path before sending

  1. Send a test to the email accounts and devices your team can check.
  2. Open the email on a phone and a desktop; confirm the thumbnail is clear and the layout still makes sense.
  3. Turn off image loading if possible. Confirm the alt text or nearby live-text link still explains the action.
  4. Click every version of the call to action and make sure it reaches the right public page.
  5. Play the video from the destination page, check captions and audio, and verify that the page matches the email promise.
  6. Review the campaign’s subject line, preview text, links, names and tracking tags.

Mailchimp recommends testing across different email clients and checking the email on mobile because rendering can vary from one client to another.[4] For the video itself, plan captions and readable text before delivery; see the accessible social video checklist for the production-side essentials.

A short pre-send checklist

  • The thumbnail accurately represents the video and links to the intended destination.
  • The email explains what the viewer will see and gives a reason to click.
  • Alt text and a nearby text link provide a useful fallback.
  • The watch page is accessible to recipients, works on mobile and includes the next step.
  • Campaign and video measurement distinguish clicks from viewing or conversion outcomes.
  • The full message has been tested in the email clients and devices available to your team.
