Video Production Call Sheets: A Practical Checklist for Business Shoots

Build one clear document that gets the client, crew, speakers and location ready for an efficient shoot day.

Published November 17, 2026Shot By Diallo editorial8 min read

A call sheet turns an approved production plan into instructions people can use on shoot day. It should answer where to go, when to arrive, who is responsible, what is being filmed and what conditions could change the plan—without exposing more personal information than the recipients need.

Start with the production identity and document status

Put the project name, client, shoot date, call-sheet version and producer contact at the top. Add the issue date and time so recipients can distinguish the current document from an older attachment.

Label the time zone when anyone is travelling or joining remotely. If the schedule changes, issue a new numbered version and summarize the change in the message that accompanies it.

Use one master schedule with individual call times

Show the overall crew call, expected wrap and the major blocks between them: access, setup, rehearsals, interviews, scenes, meal, company moves and strike. Then list individual call times for people who do not need to arrive with the full crew.

Build realistic buffers around security, elevators, parking, wardrobe, sound checks and room resets. Keep the detailed shot list separate when it would make the call sheet hard to scan, but reference the approved script or scene list by version.

Give exact location and arrival instructions

Include the full civic address, entrance, floor or room, parking or loading instructions and the on-site contact. A map pin alone may lead people to the wrong door or side of a large property.

Record reception procedures, access codes, identification requirements and restricted areas only when necessary. Confirm accessible entrances, elevators, washrooms and any accommodation already arranged. Complete the separate video location scout checklist before finalizing these details.

Publish the right contacts to the right people

List the roles needed to solve shoot-day problems: producer, director, client approver, location contact, camera, sound, styling or product lead. Use business contact information where possible.

The Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada recommends collecting only the personal information needed for a legitimate identified purpose and protecting it according to its sensitivity.[1] Apply that principle to call sheets: avoid home addresses, unnecessary personal numbers and broad distribution lists. If different groups need different details, issue a crew version and a participant version.

Translate the creative plan into shoot-day priorities

State the primary objective, the essential scenes and the priority order if time becomes limited. Identify the client representative who can approve factual details, products, spaces and spokesperson responses on site.

Mark which people, products, props, wardrobe and documents are required for each block. Avoid placing confidential script content on a widely distributed call sheet; link or attach the appropriate controlled production document instead.

Identify speakers and participants precisely

For every speaker, include the approved display name, role, arrival time, preparation location and estimated camera time. Note whether hair, makeup, wardrobe, teleprompter, accessibility support or language preparation is scheduled.

Confirm pronunciation, title spelling and bilingual delivery before the day. Use the on-camera interview preparation checklist for topic sharing, claims review, permissions and speaker comfort.

Make safety information operational

Name the person responsible for on-site production coordination and identify the location of first-aid supplies, emergency exits, assembly point and nearest appropriate medical service. The call sheet does not replace the employer’s safety obligations or the location’s emergency plan.

CNESST states that employers must ensure the required number of trained first aiders are present and provide an adequate number of compliant first-aid kits.[2] CNESST also says kits must be easy for workers to access and as close as possible to the work area.[3] Verify the applicable responsibilities with the employer, venue and qualified safety personnel rather than assuming a small crew is exempt.

Add weather and exterior contingencies

For exterior work, state the forecast location, decision time, weather owner and backup plan. Define who can pause or move the shoot and how the update will reach everyone.

Environment and Climate Change Canada provides official forecasts and active weather alerts.[4] Check it again close to call time, and do not treat a forecast captured the previous day as a safety clearance. Adjust the plan for temperature, precipitation, wind, air quality, light and travel conditions.

Separate drone operations from a casual shot request

If the plan includes a drone, identify the pilot, aircraft, operating window and weather decision, but keep sensitive certificates or operational documents with the pilot or producer rather than circulating them unnecessarily.

Transport Canada says drone pilots must follow the Canadian Aviation Regulations, survey the area for obstacles and avoid reckless or negligent operation that could endanger aviation safety or anyone.[5] A line on a call sheet does not establish that a flight is legal or safe. Use the dedicated Canadian business drone checklist and let the qualified pilot make the flight decision.

Plan meals, breaks and practical needs

Show meal timing, location and who is included. Collect dietary or accommodation information privately and share only what the responsible coordinator needs. Provide water and identify washrooms, changing space and a secure area for personal items.

Do not compress the schedule by quietly removing necessary breaks or travel time. When conditions change, update the order of scenes and inform the people affected instead of expecting everyone to infer the new plan.

List equipment and technical dependencies by exception

The full equipment inventory belongs in the production package, not necessarily on every recipient’s call sheet. Highlight the dependencies that affect the day: power access, elevator reservations, loading windows, Wi-Fi credentials managed privately, quiet periods, playback screens, special lenses or backup media.

Assign an owner to batteries, media, releases, approved products and data backup. Where the shoot produces many channel versions, connect the day’s work to the business video deliverables checklist.

Set distribution and update rules

Send the final call sheet early enough for recipients to raise conflicts, then require a simple acknowledgement from essential participants. Use a controlled link or clearly named PDF rather than pasting sensitive details into a large chat.

State who receives last-minute updates and which channel is authoritative. After the shoot, remove access when the document is no longer needed and retain only what the production and legal record requires. The Privacy Commissioner’s guidance recommends knowing what personal information is held, limiting its use and retention, and disposing of it securely.[6]

Video production call sheet checklist

  • The project, client, shoot date, version and producer are clear.
  • The time zone, crew call, individual calls and expected wrap are stated.
  • Setup, filming, breaks, moves and strike have realistic time blocks.
  • The full address, correct entrance, access and parking instructions are included.
  • Accessibility arrangements and essential location restrictions are confirmed.
  • Only necessary business contact information is distributed.
  • The shoot objective, essential scenes and priority order are stated.
  • Required speakers, products, props, wardrobe and approvers have owners.
  • Speaker names, titles, language and preparation needs are verified.
  • Emergency information, first-aid access and on-site responsibilities are confirmed.
  • Exterior work has a current weather source, decision time and backup plan.
  • Drone work is assigned to a qualified pilot with a separate compliance check.
  • Meals, water, washrooms, changing and secure storage are planned.
  • Critical equipment, power, loading and backup dependencies are identified.
  • Recipients know which version and update channel are authoritative.
  • Essential people acknowledge the final call sheet.
  • Personal information is removed or retained appropriately after production.
